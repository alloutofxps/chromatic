Palette Library

Combinations that work because someone chose them, not because a formula produced them. The Wada sets come from Sanzo Wada's 1930s dictionary; the reference sets are recurring schemes from painting, fashion and interiors.

1

2

3

Wada's colour names come from the Ridgway system he worked from. Hex values are close modern approximations — the originals were printed inks, and no digital value is exact.

Why "Refine" is on by default. Raw geometry puts every colour at identical saturation and lightness, which is exactly why generated palettes so often look cheap. Refine varies them the way a designer would — one dominant, the rest supporting. Turn it off to see the unadjusted maths.

Which colours suit you

    
Personal Colour

    
Your skin has an undertone sitting beneath its surface depth. Colours sharing that undertone make your skin look even and your eyes brighter. Colours fighting it make you look tired. Measure it with the camera, or answer six questions.

  


  

    
    
  


  
  

    

      
1
Face a window in daylight. No make-up on the sampled areas, no coloured lamp, no sunglasses.

      
2
Fill the oval with your face and hold still. Four patches are sampled: forehead, both cheeks, jaw.

      
3
Get measured values — CIELAB, ITA°, undertone angle — then a palette built from your own numbers.

    


    

      🔒
      
Nothing leaves this device. There is no server and no upload. The camera stream is read straight into a canvas, analysed in JavaScript and discarded — the only thing kept is a handful of numbers in your browser's local storage, which you can wipe at any time.

Seasonal analysis is a well-established styling heuristic, not a clinical measurement. The scan measures your skin's actual CIELAB coordinates honestly, but lighting, camera white balance, fabric and cut all still matter — and plenty of people look excellent in colours the system says they shouldn't. Treat it as a strong starting point, not a rule.
